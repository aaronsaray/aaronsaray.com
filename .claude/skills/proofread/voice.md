# Voice

What the proofread skill treats as Aaron's voice. One bullet per rule.

## Never flag

* The spaced hyphen, in all three jobs: the reversal, the aside, and
  the tack-on. "So I did that from the command line - no issues at
  all."
* Two spaces after a sentence. "I reached for validation on pagination
  requests.  This is user input."
* Trailing whitespace at the end of a line. Markdown drops it on
  render.
* A sentence or a paragraph that opens with But, And, or So, with or
  without the comma. "But, what if someone says "I want 10000" - should
  we allow that?"
* A one-line paragraph as the verdict, fragment or not. "Not forever.
  But for now, I am."
* A question tag, or a rhetorical question the next line answers.
  "Confusing, right?"
* Interjection openers: Sadly, Perfect:, Ok, Yeah, Sure. "Perfect: I'll
  go and hit one of those with an iOS shortcut and we're good to go."
* Single quotes around a term as spoken. "or even better 'start the
  last one' - that's what makes this work."
* Contractions everywhere.
* I, we, and you within one post, switching between sentences. Only a
  switch inside one sentence is an error.
* An ellipsis to trail off. "Since this is not using the simple
  positioning, I don't have the page number..."
* A parenthetical aside. "Now, you have a Laravel project running at
  localhost (or whatever you configured in your `.env` file)."
* Sentence punctuation outside the closing parenthesis, even when the
  aside is the question. "(do we really need 10k results? Won't that
  put a load on the server)?"
* Loose colloquial grammar that still parses. "AI is everywhere - and
  it has been for the last months... the last year(s)..."
* Let's and Let me as the turn into the explanation. "Let me explain
  why this happened and how I fixed it for good."
* A bold word or phrase opening a paragraph or a bullet, and the
  occasional bold line standing in for a heading. "**Fix** Of course
  we don't want to use `whereRaw()` but we must."
* An exclamation mark, including "?!". "So, why would I create this
  user with a plain text password initially?! That's crazy right?"

## How he writes

* Three modes. A how-to states the itch in a sentence or two,
  promises the fix ("Here's how."), delivers steps or a code block
  with a line of commentary after each, and stops. An opinion essay
  opens on a question or a claim, says "Let me explain", splits the
  answer under bolded run-in leads or question headings, and takes
  each in turn. A story-led explanation narrates what happened in
  first person, keeps the dead ends in, and arrives at the fix as the
  last thing tried.
* A post opens on the situation, not on background: what he was
  doing, what broke, or what he believes. A rhetorical question to
  the reader often follows, then a one-line promise before the
  excerpt break. It closes short: a clipped verdict ("Pretty
  simple."), a question handed back to the reader, or, in an essay,
  the thesis restated in one line and then widened for a paragraph.
* Paragraphs run short, and a one-sentence paragraph carries the
  weight: it lands a claim, a turn, or a reaction. Sentence length
  swings; a long explanatory sentence with a parenthetical is
  followed by a fragment or a two-word verdict. A staccato run ("I
  watch. I distill. I formulate.") marks the peak of an essay, not
  its filler.
* Vocabulary is spoken and contracted. Technical terms arrive with no
  ceremony when the reader is assumed to share them, and by analogy
  when not (wiring a house, the mechanic who opens a shop). Spoken
  interjections sit in the prose: "eek", "Hrm", "Naw", "Ugh", "Heh",
  "sigh". Humor is a quick inline aside, a jab at a tool, or a
  self-deprecating note, and the paragraph moves on.
* He addresses the reader as "you", reports what he did as "I", and
  switches to "we" when walking through code together. He positions
  himself by experience rather than title and hedges his own fix
  openly (it works for him, he is not sure it is the right one) while
  inviting correction.
* Code blocks are short and scoped to the point: a sentence before
  says what the block is for, a sentence or three after say what to
  notice. Identifiers, settings, paths, and commands are inline code.
  Steps are bulleted with UI labels in bold. Screenshots drop in as
  progress markers and are not described further.
* An argument states the claim first, then the principles he holds,
  then checks the case against each under a bolded lead or a question
  heading. Evidence is his own experience and a worked example, never
  a citation. A concession is explicit and one sentence long
  ("Contractors are not bad people."), and the "But" that follows is
  the point. The thesis returns at the end in fewer words than it had
  at the start.
* What reads as an AI tell but is his: tidy parallel structure (three
  bolded principles, each checked in turn), a confident declarative
  that opens or closes a paragraph ("You need a technical
  cofounder."), a neat closing line, a rule-of-three list, "Let me
  explain" as a promise, a rhetorical question answered on the next
  line, and the "Not X. But Y." beat. What separates his from
  generated text is what surrounds these: an "I" in nearly every
  paragraph, a fragment or interjection within reach, and a concrete
  tool or moment named in the same breath.

## Sounds like him

* "Easy to use tech doesn't mean tech is easy - it means someone really
  invested in that tech."
* "Your idea may be magnificent, but ideas are easy. (I'm sorry to burst
  your bubble.)"
* "My pattern is to watch something, assess it the first time, and then
  return to it later to assess it again. I'm not a hype man and I'm not
  a hater."
* "I also know that if I don't make this automated, I won't do it. So,
  I automated the process. Here's how I did it."
* "Sadly that's not the case. They're only progress widgets.  So, after
  doing some research, I created my own iOS shortcut.  Here's how."
* "Ok - fine. This just shows me the **2025-06-18 10:00:00** again. I
  know it's a time, though."
* "There's a lot to look at here, even though it's very simple. Let's
  dive in."
* "I'm not anti AI." and, further down, "So I'm waiting."
* "That's it. Now, you have a Laravel project running at localhost (or
  whatever you configured in your `.env` file)."
* "*Is this the right solution?* Is there a better one? Let me know!"
* "I don't know." as a whole paragraph.

## Reads as generated

* A real em dash or en dash, and curly quotes.
* delve, crucial, seamless, leverage, tapestry, landscape.
* "It's not about X, it's about Y" and the wider contrast frame.
* "not just X, but Y" when it recurs in one post.
* Three parallel clauses with a rising rhythm, when they recur.
* Additionally, Moreover, Furthermore as sentence openers.
* "Here's the thing", "Let's be clear", "In conclusion", "Ultimately",
  "In today's".
* A closing paragraph that recaps the whole post, as opposed to his
  one-line restatement of the thesis.
* A heading over every paragraph.
* Stacked hedges in one sentence: may, often, in many cases.
